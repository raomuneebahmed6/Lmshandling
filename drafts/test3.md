<h2 id="check-steps">How do I check my VU date sheet?</h2>

Your VU date sheet is a personal schedule of papers, and it is published on a separate datesheet portal, not in your normal VULMS dashboard. Students who only look inside VULMS often conclude it has not been released. The order below avoids that confusion. For the long version with portal details, see our [VU datesheet guide](/blog/vu-datesheet-guide).

1. **Find the official portal address.** Take it from VU's own website or official announcement, not from a forwarded link or a third-party page.
2. **Sign in with your VULMS ID and password.** The same credentials you use on VULMS normally apply.
3. **Select your exam city if the portal asks.** Pick the city where you can actually travel on paper day.
4. **Open the schedule for your registered courses.** Each course you registered for should appear with its own paper.
5. **Save a screenshot or print.** Keep a copy in case the page is slow or unavailable later.

The portal layout can change between exam cycles, so if a step looks different, follow what the page says and confirm anything unclear with VU.

<h2 id="what-to-verify">What should I verify once I see it?</h2>

Seeing a schedule is not the same as having checked it. Spend two minutes on each of these:

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Course code and name | Make sure every registered course appears and none is missing |
| Date | Look for two papers on the same day or very close together |
| Time | Papers can be in different sessions, so note each one separately |
| City and center | Confirm the address and how long the journey takes |
| Your name and ID | Make sure the schedule belongs to your account |

If a course is missing or the city looks wrong, report it to VU through its official support channels early. Waiting until exam week leaves very little time for anything to be corrected.

<h2 id="not-showing">What if the date sheet is not showing?</h2>

An empty or missing date sheet is common in the days before a release, and it does not always mean something is wrong with your account. Work through these in order:

- **Check whether it has been released.** Look at VU's official notices and our [VU important dates](/blog/vu-important-dates-2026) page. Schedules for different exams are often released at different times.
- **Confirm your course registration.** If a course is not registered on VULMS, it will not appear on the date sheet.
- **Re-check your login.** Use your own VULMS ID and a current password, and try again later if the portal is busy.
- **Try another device or browser.** A cached page can hide an updated schedule.
- **Contact VU.** If everything above checks out and the release has happened, reach out through VU's official support contacts and include your VULMS ID and the course codes involved.

For an example of how a past release was announced, see the [Summer Semester finals datesheet](/blog/vu-summer-semester-finals-datesheet-2026) post.

<h2 id="plan-around-it">Plan your preparation around the dates</h2>

The date sheet is most useful as a planning tool. Once you have it, count backwards from each paper:

- **Mark every paper on a calendar** and highlight any two papers within a day or two of each other.
- **Give the hardest course the earliest start,** not the last slot before the paper.
- **Split each course into lectures and past topics,** so each study session has a clear target.
- **Leave the last day for revision only,** and do a short travel check for the center.

Our [midterm and finalterm preparation guide](/blog/vu-midterm-finalterm-exam-preparation-guide) goes through how to divide Objective and Subjective revision, and the [VU Notes](/vu-notes) page lists the course codes with available files. A date sheet cannot tell you what will come in a paper, so build your plan around the syllabus and your own revision, and treat any outcome as dependent on your preparation.

<h3 id="paper-week">Paper week routine</h3>

In the week before your first paper, re-open the date sheet once more. Details can be updated, and a quick second look catches a changed time or center before it becomes a problem. Then run through a short list:

- **Confirm the center address** and plan the route, including how early you need to leave.
- **Keep your copy of the schedule** and your ID ready the night before.
- **Check each paper's time separately,** since a morning and an evening paper on the same day need different planning.
- **Rest properly.** Cramming a new topic the night before rarely helps more than a calm revision of what you already covered.

If you are handling several courses in one cycle, write the papers in order of date, not in order of course code. It makes the whole schedule far easier to read at a glance and helps you spot a heavy week early enough to rearrange your revision.
