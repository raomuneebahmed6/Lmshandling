<h2 id="gdb-format">What a well structured GDB post looks like</h2>

A Graded Discussion Board (GDB) post on VULMS is typed directly into the course's discussion thread. There is no file to upload like an assignment, and the post is visible to the rest of the class once it is submitted. A GDB is graded on how directly the post answers the exact question asked, not on how much a student types, and three parts consistently separate a high scoring post from an average one:

- **An opening line that states a clear position.** The grader should be able to tell the student's stance from the first sentence, not guess it from a general discussion of the topic.
- **Two or three supporting points tied to the lecture.** Each point should reference a specific concept, term, or example from the course content rather than restating a generic opinion.
- **A short closing line.** One sentence that restates the position, so the post reads as a complete answer rather than stopping mid thought.

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Mistakes that break the format</h2>

Most GDB posts that lose marks are not wrong about the topic, they just do not follow a format a grader can quickly assess. The most common issues are a post with no stated position at all, a wall of text with no visible break between points, and a post that drifts into general commentary on the topic instead of answering the specific question on the page. Reading the GDB prompt twice before writing, and checking the draft against it once more before posting, catches most of these issues on its own.

<h2 id="plagiarism-deadline">Plagiarism and deadline rules</h2>

Because every student's GDB post sits on the same visible thread, VU checks posts for similarity across the whole class. A post that is copied, closely paraphrased, or lifted from an online source, including a classmate's earlier post, is typically marked zero, regardless of when it was submitted. The safest approach is writing the post in the student's own words based on their own reading of the lecture material.

GDB windows are also short by design, usually just a few days, and the thread locks automatically once the deadline passes. There is generally no extension for a missed GDB, so the opening date of the window, not the closing date, is the one worth treating as the real deadline. Always confirm the exact opening and closing times for a specific GDB on VULMS, since the window can vary by course and semester.
